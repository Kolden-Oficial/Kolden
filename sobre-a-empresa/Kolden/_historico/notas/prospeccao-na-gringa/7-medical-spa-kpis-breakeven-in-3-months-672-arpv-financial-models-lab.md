#### KPI 1 : Average Visits Per Day (AVPD)

##### Definition

Average Visits Per Day (AVPD) tells you the raw volume of clients walking through the door, calculated by dividing total visits by the number of days you were open. This metric is crucial because it directly dictates your day-to-day operational capacity needs, like scheduling nurses and aestheticians. For Elysian Aesthetics & Wellness, the target is hitting **12 visits/day in 2026** , which you need to watch daily to manage staffing levels.

##### Advantages

```
*  Directly links operational load to staffing needs.
*  Helps forecast daily supply usage for treatments.
*  Shows if marketing efforts translate into immediate foot traffic.
```

##### Disadvantages

```
*  Ignores the value of each visit (ARPV is separate).
*  Doesn't account for room utilization or appointment length.
*  A high number might mask poor scheduling if rooms sit empty.
```

##### Industry Benchmarks

Benchmarks vary widely based on clinic size and service mix. A small, specialized clinic might see 5 to 8 daily visits, while larger centers targeting high throughput might aim for 20+. Tracking your AVPD against your **2026 target of 12** helps you confirm you are scaling capacity appropriately for your premium model.

##### How To Improve

```
*  Implement targeted campaigns to fill mid-week appointment slots.
*  Improve client retention to increase frequency of return visits.
*  Optimize scheduling software to reduce gaps between appointments.
```

##### How To Calculate

You calculate AVPD by taking the total number of clients you served in a period and dividing it by the number of days the facility was operational. This gives you the average daily load you must staff for.
AVPD = Total Visits / Operating Days

##### Example of Calculation

To see if you hit your 2026 goal, you divide the total number of clients seen by the days you were open. If you saw **300 visits** over **25 operating days** in a given month, your AVPD is 12, matching the goal set for that year.
AVPD = 300 Visits / 25 Days = 12 Visits/Day

##### Tips and Trics

```
*  Review AVPD every morning to adjust same-day staffing.
*  Segment AVPD by service type to see which treatments drive volume.
*  Compare actual AVPD against the  **12 visits/day**  forecast.
*  Use AVPD trends to defintely negotiate better supply pricing based on volume commitments.
```

#### KPI 2 : Average Revenue Per Visit (ARPV)

##### Definition

Average Revenue Per Visit (ARPV) tells you exactly how much money you generate every time a client comes in for service or a purchase. It is the primary measure of revenue efficiency, showing how well you monetize each appointment slot. For your medical spa in 2026, you are aiming for a combined ARPV of **$59,330** , built from $59,250 service ARPV and $80 retail ARPV, defintely reviewed on a weekly basis.

##### Advantages

```
*  Shows the immediate financial impact of your service pricing structure.
*  Directly measures the success of attaching retail sales to service appointments.
*  Helps you prioritize scheduling high-value clients over low-value ones.
```

##### Disadvantages

```
*  It hides the true volume of visits needed to hit revenue goals.
*  It doesn't account for the high Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) on services.
*  A high ARPV might mask poor Treatment Room Utilization Rate if visits are too long.
```

##### Industry Benchmarks

For aesthetic practices, ARPV must be high enough to cover specialized physician supervision and expensive supplies. Your 2026 target suggests a heavy reliance on high-ticket procedures, which is typical for a luxury medical spa model. You need to compare this figure against peers who offer similar injectable and laser packages, not standard day spas.

##### How To Improve

```
*  Actively shift the service mix to increase revenue from Body Contouring, targeting the  **25%**  goal.
*  Implement mandatory retail consultations to push the retail ARPV above the  **$80**  baseline.
*  Review weekly data to ensure staff aren't prioritizing low-margin services just to keep volume up.
```

##### How To Calculate

To find your ARPV, you simply divide your total revenue earned over a period by the total number of visits that occurred during that same period. This works whether you are looking at monthly, quarterly, or annual results.
ARPV = Total Revenue / Total Visits

##### Example of Calculation

If you are analyzing your projected 2026 performance, you look at the components that make up the total revenue generated per visit. The target ARPV is the sum of the expected service revenue and the expected retail revenue for that single visit.
Projected ARPV (2026) = $59,250 (Service ARPV) + $80 (Retail ARPV) = $59,330
This calculation confirms that your efficiency target is heavily weighted toward the high-value service component.

##### Tips and Trics

```
*  Segment ARPV by service type to see which procedures drive the most revenue.
*  Track ARPV alongside Average Visits Per Day (AVPD) to balance volume and value.
*  If ARPV drops, immediately check if Treatment Room Utilization Rate is suffering.
*  Use the weekly review cycle to adjust staffing based on expected revenue density per day.
```

#### KPI 3 : High-Value Service Mix %

##### Definition

High-Value Service Mix Percentage tracks how much of your total revenue comes from premium, high-priced aesthetic treatments. This is critical because these services, like **Body Contouring** , often carry significantly better gross margins than standard offerings. We need to see this mix climb from **20%** today to **25%** by 2030, and we check this number monthly.

##### Advantages

```
*  Directly measures success in upselling core, high-margin procedures.
*  Validates the market acceptance of premium pricing, like the  **$1,200**  Body Contouring service.
*  Focuses operational attention on the services that drive EBITDA growth.
```

##### Disadvantages

```
*  Creates dependency on a small number of high-ticket procedures.
*  Can mask underlying issues if standard service revenue is declining.
*  Requires specialized provider training, increasing onboarding complexity.
```

##### Industry Benchmarks

For specialized medical spas focused on advanced treatments, a mix above **20%** is generally expected to cover high fixed costs like physician supervision. If you operate in a saturated market, top performers often push this mix toward **30%** or higher. You can't sustain high overheads if the majority of your revenue comes from low-cost retail add-ons.

##### How To Improve

```
*  Incentivize providers based on the dollar volume of premium services sold.
*  Mandate that all new clients receive a consultation for the  **$1,200**  Body Contouring service.
*  Create tiered service packages where the premium option is the default starting point.
```

##### How To Calculate

To find this percentage, take the total revenue generated specifically from your premium services and divide it by your total gross revenue for the period. Then multiply by 100 to get the percentage.
(Revenue from Premium Services / Total Revenue) x 100

##### Example of Calculation

Say in a given month, your total revenue was **$150,000** . If you sold **$33,000** worth of high-value treatments like Body Contouring, you calculate the mix like this:
($33,000 / $150,000) x 100 = **22%**
This means **22%** of your revenue came from the high-value bucket, which is slightly ahead of the pace needed to hit the **2030** goal.

##### Tips and Trics

```
*  Segment revenue streams by service tier defintely, not just by total dollars.
*  Model the financial impact of increasing the mix by just  **1%**  annually.
*  Tie provider bonuses directly to the mix percentage, not just total service volume.
*  If the mix drops below  **20%**  for two consecutive months, flag it for immediate leadership review.
```

#### KPI 4 : Treatment Room Utilization Rate

##### Definition

Treatment Room Utilization Rate measures how efficiently you use your physical space by comparing time spent treating clients against total time the room is ready for service. For a medical spa, this KPI directly impacts how well you cover high fixed costs like rent and specialized equipment leases. You must target **70% or higher** , reviewing this metric weekly to catch scheduling inefficiencies fast.

##### Advantages

```
*  Identifies revenue capacity limits before needing new physical space.
*  Shows scheduling patterns that leave prime slots empty, allowing targeted promotions.
*  Lowers the effective fixed cost burden carried by each service performed.
```

##### Disadvantages

```
*  Rates below target mean you are paying for idle, expensive square footage.
*  Sustained utilization above  **90%**  often signals staff burnout risk or poor client flow management.
*  It doesn't differentiate between a quick injectable appointment and a long laser session.
```

##### Industry Benchmarks

For aesthetic practices relying heavily on specialized rooms, anything consistently under **60%** utilization is a red flag signaling poor operational leverage. The goal of **70%** is a healthy operational standard that allows room for necessary cleaning, setup time, and physician consultation buffers. If your facility is running at **85%** , you're likely maximizing asset use, but you must watch client wait times closely.

##### How To Improve

```
*  Use the weekly review to immediately shift provider schedules into low-utilization zones.
*  Incentivize providers to book shorter, high-margin services during utilization dips.
*  Implement automated reminders to reduce no-shows, which instantly create empty, unused hours.
```

##### How To Calculate

You calculate this by dividing the total time rooms were actively used for treatments by the total time those rooms were scheduled to be open for business. This gives you a clear percentage of efficiency. It's defintely important to track this against your Average Visits Per Day (AVPD) target of **12 visits/day** in 2026.

##### Example of Calculation

Imagine your facility operates 5 days a week, with 10 available hours each day for treatments, totaling **50 Available Hours** . Last week, your providers logged **38 Actual Treatment Hours** across all rooms. We plug those numbers into the formula to see the utilization percentage.
Treatment Room Utilization Rate = 38 Actual Treatment Hours / 50 Available Hours
This results in a utilization rate of **0.76** , or **76%** for the week, which is above your 70% goal.

##### Tips and Trics

```
*  Track utilization by individual provider, not just facility-wide.
*  Factor in  **15 minutes**  buffer time between appointments for cleaning/prep.
*  Compare utilization against your High-Value Service Mix % monthly.
*  If utilization lags, focus marketing spend on filling specific slow day slots.
```

#### KPI 5 : Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) %

##### Definition

Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) Percentage shows how much money you spend directly making the revenue you earn. For this medical spa, it tracks the cost of injectables and retail items against total sales. Keeping this number low directly boosts your gross profit margin, which is key for scaling.

##### Advantages

```
*  Pinpoints rising supply costs immediately.
*  Helps set profitable service prices.
*  Shows inventory waste or inefficiency.
```

##### Disadvantages

```
*  Ignores fixed operating expenses like rent.
*  Can fluctuate based on inventory accounting methods.
*  Doesn't reflect labor efficiency in service delivery.
```

##### Industry Benchmarks

For specialized medical services, COGS % is often lower than standard retail, maybe **20% to 40%** , depending on the service mix. High-end aesthetic practices aim for COGS below **35%** to protect high service margins. If your costs are higher, you're leaving money on the table.

##### How To Improve

```
*  Renegotiate bulk purchasing agreements for injectables.
*  Reduce slow-moving retail stock to cut holding costs.
*  Increase the mix of high-value services that use lower relative supplies.
```

##### How To Calculate

You calculate COGS % by taking your total supply costs and dividing them by your total revenue for the period. This metric must be reviewed monthly to catch deviations fast.
(Total Cost of Injectables + Total Cost of Retail Purchases) / Total Revenue

##### Example of Calculation

In 2026, your supply costs are projected based on their mix: **50%** for injectables and **20%** for retail purchases. If your total revenue for a month hits $100,000, your total COGS is $70,000 based on those components. We need to drive that down to **53%** by 2030.
($50,000 Injectables + $20,000 Retail) / $100,000 Revenue = **70% COGS**

##### Tips and Trics

```
*  Track injectable costs separately from retail purchases.
*  Review the percentage monthly against the  **53%**  2030 goal.
*  Ensure retail markups cover holding costs and shrinkage.
*  Factor in waste from expired medical supplies defintely.
```

#### KPI 6 : Labor Cost as % of Revenue

##### Definition

Labor Cost as % of Revenue measures how much of your sales revenue is consumed by payroll expenses, including fixed salaries. This ratio is a primary indicator of operating leverage; when it's high, you are paying too much in fixed overhead relative to the work you are doing. For this medical spa, the current **131%** ratio means fixed labor costs exceed revenue, which is unsustainable past the initial ramp-up phase.

##### Advantages

```
*  Shows exactly how much revenue growth is needed to cover fixed wages.
*  Highlights the urgency of increasing Average Visits Per Day (AVPD).
*  Forces disciplined review of staffing levels every month.
```

##### Disadvantages

```
*  A high starting percentage is expected but can mask underlying revenue generation issues.
*  It doesn't account for variable labor costs, like commissions paid to providers.
*  If revenue projections are overly optimistic, this ratio will remain dangerously high.
```

##### Industry Benchmarks

For specialized service providers like high-end medical spas, labor costs should ideally settle between **30% and 40%** of revenue once operations are mature and utilizing high Average Revenue Per Visit (ARPV). A ratio starting at 131% means the business must achieve significant volume quickly to absorb the **$275,000** fixed wage base planned for 2026.

##### How To Improve

```
*  Focus all energy on scaling AVPD from 12 to  **35**  visits daily.
*  Ensure that revenue growth is driven by high-value services to increase the revenue denominator faster.
*  Review fixed staffing allocations monthly against actual daily visit throughput.
```

##### How To Calculate

You calculate this ratio by dividing your total fixed wages by your total revenue for the period, then multiplying by 100 to get a percentage. This metric is most useful when comparing the fixed cost burden across different operational scales.
Labor Cost as % of Revenue = (Total Fixed Wages / Total Revenue) x 100

##### Example of Calculation

If the planned fixed wages for 2026 are **$275,000** annually, and the current revenue only supports a **131%** ratio, we can back into the current revenue base. This calculation shows the immediate operational gap that must be closed by increasing patient flow.
Revenue = $275,000 / 1.31 = $209,923 (Annual Revenue)

##### Tips and Trics

```
*  Determine the exact revenue needed to bring the ratio down to  **50%** .
*  Model the revenue impact of scaling from 12 to  **35**  visits per day.
*  Use the monthly review cycle to adjust variable scheduling, not fixed headcount.
*  Track the blended ARPV closely; higher ARPV reduces the labor percentage faster.
```

#### KPI 7 : EBITDA Margin

##### Definition

EBITDA Margin measures core profitability. It tells you how much operating profit you generate for every dollar of revenue before accounting for interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (non-cash charges). For your medical spa, this is the key metric showing how efficiently you convert service and retail sales into cash profit from operations. The plan requires strong growth here, targeting an increase from **$893,000** in Year 1 up to **$5,743,000** by Year 5, and you must review this result monthly.

##### Advantages

```
*  It strips out financing decisions (debt/interest) and tax strategies, focusing purely on operational efficiency.
*  It directly tracks progress toward the  **$5.7M**  Year 5 profitability goal, irrespective of asset age or tax structure.
*  It forces management to control variable costs like supplies (COGS) and labor relative to sales volume.
```

##### Disadvantages

```
*  It ignores capital intensity; high-cost laser equipment requires depreciation that this metric skips over.
*  It can mask poor cash management if high revenue growth relies heavily on delayed payments.
*  It doesn't reflect the actual cash flow available to service debt or fund owner distributions.
```

##### Industry Benchmarks

For specialized medical services, mature, efficient operations often see EBITDA margins settling between **20% and 35%** . If your Year 5 revenue scales significantly, maintaining a margin that supports $5.7M in EBITDA means you must keep operating costs tight, especially as you scale past the initial Year 1 fixed labor burden of $275,000. Benchmarks help you see if your cost structure is competitive as you grow.

##### How To Improve

```
*  Increase the  **High-Value Service Mix %** , as premium treatments usually carry better gross margins than standard retail.
*  Aggressively manage  **COGS %**  by locking in better pricing for injectable supplies and skincare inventory.
*  Improve  **Treatment Room Utilization Rate**  to maximize revenue generated per hour without adding fixed overhead.
```

##### How To Calculate

To find the margin, you take your operating profit before non-cash items and divide it by your total sales. This shows the percentage of revenue that is pure operating earnings.
EBITDA Margin = (EBITDA / Total Revenue)

##### Example of Calculation

Say by Year 5, you project total revenue hitting **$15,000,000** . To hit your target EBITDA of **$5,743,000** , you calculate the required margin. This tells you exactly how efficient you need to be operationally to meet the growth plan.
EBITDA Margin = ($5,743,000 / $15,000,000) = 38.29%

##### Tips and Trics

```
*  Track this metric  **monthly** ; daily or weekly tracking is too noisy for this high-level metric.
*  Watch how the  **Labor Cost as % of Revenue**  changes; if it stays hig
```
