### 2025 Healthcare Marketing Benchmarks

You can find 2026 benchmark updates here.
Benchmarks for medical practices, clinics, and healthcare brands to evaluate performance and plan smarter marketing in 2025.
Healthcare marketing is under more pressure than ever. Patients are researching providers online, reading reviews, comparing options, and expecting easy digital access to care. At the same time, advertising costs are rising and competition is increasing in many specialties.
This 2025 benchmark report brings together current healthcare marketing statistics and channel benchmarks so practices can understand what “normal” looks like. Use it to compare your performance, identify gaps, and make more confident decisions about budgets and strategy.

#### 1. Why Benchmarks Matter in Healthcare

Healthcare is different from most other verticals. Patients are not just choosing a product. They are trusting you with their health. That makes trust, access, and experience just as important as cost per click.
Benchmarks help you answer questions like:
\* “Are our Google Ads campaigns performing competitively for our specialty?”
\* “Is our website converting enough visitors into inquiries and appointments?”
\* “Are our email and content efforts in line with other providers?”
\* “How much should practices like ours expect to spend per lead?”
Industry benchmarks do not replace your own KPIs. They provide a directional “reality check” to complement your internal data.

#### 2. Patient Behavior and Demand Benchmarks

Before looking at channel metrics, it is important to understand patient behavior online.
\* **77% of patients search online before booking an appointment.**
\* **About 68% say online reviews influence which provider they choose.**
\* **Roughly three in four patients look for providers on Google Maps or local search.**
In other words, search engines and reviews now sit at the center of the modern patient journey. Healthcare digital ad spend in the United States is projected to reach roughly $19.7 billion in 2025, with strong growth focused on search, display, and paid social for healthcare brands and providers. [1]

#### 3. Search Advertising Benchmarks for Healthcare (2025)

Many patients begin their journey with a search question. “Primary care doctor near me.” “Urgent care open now.” “Pediatric dentist in [city].” Search advertising lets you appear at the exact moment that question is asked.

##### 3.1 Cross-Industry vs Healthcare Benchmarks

Across all industries in 2025, search advertising benchmarks from large data sets show roughly: [2]
\* **Average click-through rate (CTR), all industries:** about 6.7%
\* **Average cost per click (CPC), all industries:** about $5.26
\* **Average conversion rate (CVR), all industries:** just over 7.5%
\* **Average cost per lead (CPL), all industries:** about $70
Healthcare-specific benchmarks look different. In a healthcare-focused analysis that covers multiple healthcare subcategories, typical search performance for hospitals, clinics, dental practices, home care, gyms, and related categories includes: [3]
\* **CTR:** often between about 5% and 8% for most healthcare subcategories
\* **CPC:** commonly in the range of roughly $3 to $7 per click, depending on service line
\* **CPL:** ranging from about $16 on the low end (for categories like massage therapy) up to $70 or more for higher-cost services such as diet and weight loss or hearing care
Many medical and dental practices actually see lower CPCs than legal or home services campaigns. The real pressure in healthcare is often on conversion and response speed rather than the click cost itself.

##### 3.2 Practical Search Targets for Healthcare Providers

For a well-structured search program in healthcare, reasonable starting targets often include:
\* CTR at or above **5%–7%** for core service lines
\* CPC roughly in the **$3–$6** range for many outpatient and clinic services, higher for certain specialties and competitive metros
\* Conversion rate at or above **7%–10%** for appointment or inquiry focused campaigns with solid landing pages
\* CPL comfortably below the average profit margin for a new patient or treatment plan
If your practice is far below these targets for comparable services and markets, it may be time to review your keyword targeting, ad copy, landing page experience, and intake process.

#### 4. Social, Display, and Awareness Benchmarks

Not every patient books right after a search. Many need time, education, or repeated exposure. This is where social and display come in.

##### 4.1 Healthcare Social Advertising Benchmarks

Healthcare social ad data across hospitals, clinics, home care, gyms, and other subcategories shows: [3]
\* **Typical social CTR:** roughly 1.0% to 1.9%
\* **Average cost per click:** often around $1 to $2
\* **CPL:** varying widely by offer, often in the $60–$120 range for many healthcare categories
These numbers are in line with or better than broader cross-industry social benchmarks, where CTR often sits below 1% and CPC can be slightly higher for some verticals. [4]

##### 4.2 Healthcare Display Advertising Benchmarks

Display ads for healthcare typically focus on education and awareness. Benchmark data for healthcare display campaigns shows: [3]
\* **CTR:** often near or slightly above 0.1%
\* **CPC:** frequently in the $3–$6 range
\* **CPL:** varies. Some subcategories such as home care see relatively low CPLs compared to other display categories
Because display is top-of-funnel, the primary success metric is usually cost-effective reach and assisted conversions, not last-click leads.

#### 5. Local SEO and Reviews Benchmarks for Healthcare

Local search is especially important in healthcare. Patients want providers nearby, covered by their insurance, and trusted by other patients. Google Business Profiles and online reviews heavily influence that decision.
Key benchmarks and expectations:
\* **Discovery via local search:** with roughly 77% of patients searching online before booking, local visibility is now a critical acquisition channel
\* **Review influence:** around 68% of patients say reviews influence which provider they choose
\* **Local search behavior:** about three in four patients look for providers on Google Maps or similar local interfaces [2][3]
Rather than chasing one universal “good” number, healthcare organizations should benchmark:
\* Year-over-year growth in calls, website visits, and direction requests from Google Business Profiles
\* Review volume and average star rating compared to local competitors
\* Number of recent reviews. Recency is often as important as volume

#### 6. Website and Conversion Benchmarks for Healthcare

Even with strong PPC and local visibility, the patient experience on your website and landing pages often determines whether a visitor becomes an inquiry or a lost opportunity.

##### 6.1 Experience Benchmarks

High-performing healthcare sites usually share common traits:
\* Fast, mobile-friendly pages with clear navigation
\* Prominent calls to action for “Request an appointment” or “Call now”
\* Visible reviews, ratings, and trust signals
\* Clear explanations of services, insurance, and locations
Because many patients search and book from mobile, page speed and mobile usability should be treated as non-negotiable benchmarks rather than nice-to-have improvements.

##### 6.2 Conversion Benchmarks

Conversion rate expectations vary by specialty and appointment type. Industry research and performance data from medical practices suggest:
\* **Healthy range for many healthcare landing pages:** about 5% to 10% of paid search visitors converting to a tracked inquiry or appointment request
\* **Higher rates:** possible for urgent-care style services and low-friction booking flows
\* **Lower but still acceptable rates:** often seen for specialty care where patients compare options or need referrals
On the practice side, internal conversion is often the real bottleneck. Some studies suggest that only about one out of nine inquiries becomes an actual patient for many medical practices, often due to slow follow up, missed calls, or complex scheduling. [5]
This means that improving intake speed and follow up often has as much impact as optimizing ads or landing pages.

#### 7. Email and Content Benchmarks for Healthcare

Email and educational content are powerful tools for both patient acquisition and retention. Healthcare email campaigns perform slightly above average compared to other industries.
Recent healthcare-focused benchmark data shows: [1]
\* **Average email open rate:** about 22.6% in healthcare
\* **Average click-through rate:** around 2.8%
\* Both metrics sit above cross-industry averages, which are closer to 21% open and about 2.3% click rates
Patient education topics, preventive care tips, and personalized reminders typically perform better than purely promotional messages.

#### 8. Budget and Channel Mix Benchmarks for Healthcare

Healthcare budgets vary widely by organization type. A solo practice, multi-location group, and regional hospital system will not operate at the same scale. However, there are recurring patterns.

##### 8.1 Typical Monthly Ad Spend Ranges

```
*   **Small independent practices:**  often in the range of $1,500 to $4,000 per month on digital ads across search, social, and display
*   **Mid-sized groups and multi-location practices:**  commonly $4,000 to $10,000+ per month depending on specialties and service areas
*   **Hospitals and large systems:**  significantly higher budgets, typically structured by service line and market
```

##### 8.2 Common Channel Mix Patterns

```
*   **Search + Local SEO:**  core for provider discovery and appointment intent
*   **Social + Display:**  used for branding, education, and remarketing
*   **Email + content:**  used for retention, education, and follow up
*   **Video:**  increasingly used to explain procedures and introduce providers
```

The most effective mix is usually the one that aligns budget with the channels that are already producing measurable, high-value appointments.

#### 9. How to Use These Benchmarks in Your Organization

Benchmarks are only useful if they lead to decisions. A few practical applications:
\* **Quarterly performance reviews:** compare your CTR, CPC, CPL, and conversion rates to healthcare averages, not just cross-industry numbers
\* **Spot red flags:** if you are far off benchmark ranges, decide whether the issue is targeting, creative, landing page experience, or intake
\* **Document your own baselines:** track your metrics over time so you can build internal benchmarks for each service line
\* **Align teams:** use benchmarks to have clear conversations between marketing, operations, and clinical leadership about what is realistic and what needs improvement
Over time, your own data becomes the most valuable benchmark of all. Industry numbers show you where the bar is. Your internal trends show you how far you have come and where you can go next.
