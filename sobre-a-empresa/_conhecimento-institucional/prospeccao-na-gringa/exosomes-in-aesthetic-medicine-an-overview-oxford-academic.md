The use of exosomes in regenerative aesthetics represents a conceptual shift. Although stem cells operate using an engraftment and differentiation method, exosomes mediate therapeutic effects through transfer of bioactive molecules that orchestrate regenerative cascades. These tiny particles have evolved from their initial characterization as cellular waste repositories to critical mediators of physiological processes. Laboratory research and clinical trials position exosomes as tailored biological messengers with tissue-specific tropism and immunomodulatory capabilities. Sourcing diversity, ranging from mesenchymal stem cells to immune cells and platelets, imparts distinct molecular signatures that determine therapeutic potential. Contemporary isolation techniques have progressed from ultracentrifugation to more sophisticated approaches yielding variable purity profiles. Regulatory frameworks span Section 351 of the Public Health Service Act governing minimally manipulated preparations, Section 361 therapeutics with specific drug indications, and Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act–regulated topical formulations, with international approaches varying significantly across jurisdictions. Clinical applications include photoaging remediation, scar remodeling, hair follicle regeneration, wound healing, postprocedure recovery and adipose tissue volumization, with mechanisms encompassing matrix remodeling, neovascularization, and immunomodulation. Emerging evidence supports efficacy in combination with energy-based treatments and microneedling. Future directions include cargo-engineered exosomes, stimuli-responsive release systems, and tissue-specific targeting. Developing GMP-compliant production and unified potency metrics will likely transition exosomes from supplementary components to primary agents in next-generation aesthetic protocols.

#### THE EMERGENCE OF EXOSOMES IN AESTHETIC MEDICINE

Exosomes—nanoscale extracellular vesicles ranging from 30 to 150 nm that facilitate cell-to-cell communication—have emerged as transformative tools in aesthetic medicine, bridging the gap between traditional skincare and traditional definitive procedures.1 These biological messengers carry complex cargoes of proteins, lipids, and nucleic acids that modulate recipient-cell behavior through multiple signaling pathways, effectively recapturing the rejuvenating effects observed in groundbreaking parabiosis research.2 Exosomes are but a single member of the secretome family, a group of endogenous secretions that are mobilized in response to bodily stress or injury.3 The scientific rationale for exosome-based therapies, especially those derived from mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs), stems from their unique ability to deliver comprehensive regenerative signals without the challenges of cell-based approaches.4 In aesthetic applications, exosomes demonstrate remarkable versatility, addressing multiple facets of aging simultaneously: reversing fibroblast senescence, normalizing extracellular-matrix production, modulating inflammatory responses, and activating resident stem-cell populations.5,6 Clinical applications have rapidly expanded to facial rejuvenation, where exosomes stimulate collagen synthesis and dermal reorganization; hair restoration through targeted activation of dermal papilla cells; scar management by modulating the transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β)/Smad pathway; and accelerated recovery following energy-based treatments. Each application leverages exosomes’ fundamental ability to restore youthful cellular function through paracrine signaling.7 Although current exosome products vary in preparation methods, storage requirements, and regulatory status (Table 1), the field is advancing toward greater standardization and enhanced efficacy. The horizon includes engineered exosomes with designer cargoes, targeted delivery mechanisms, and precision formulations that promise significant results.8,9 Because research accelerates and regulatory frameworks evolve, exosomes represent a compelling frontier in aesthetic medicine—one that harnesses intrinsic regenerative mechanisms to achieve natural-looking results with minimal downtime.

#### BASICS OF EXOSOMES: BIOGENESIS, DOCKING, AND RECIPIENT MODIFICATION

Exosomes are nano-sized extracellular vesicles secreted by virtually all cell types through endosomal pathway maturation and multivesicular body (MVB) fusion with plasma membranes.10 The fundamental difference between stem cells and exosomes lies in their mechanism of action: stem cells require engraftment and survival within host tissues, whereas exosomes deliver bioactive cargo without cellular persistence concerns.11 Structurally, exosomes possess a phospholipid bilayer enriched with tetraspanins (CD9, CD63, and CD81), adhesion molecules, and membrane transport proteins that facilitate targeted cellular interactions. Their interior contains a sophisticated molecular payload including proteins, enzymes, lipids, metabolites, and nucleic acids (messenger RNAs [mRNAs], microRNAs [miRNAs], and long noncoding RNA [lncRNAs]) that collectively modulate recipient-cell behavior without the genomic integration risks associated with cellular therapies (Figure 1).12,13 This distinction represents a mechanistic shift offering advantages in advantages in stability, ease of storage and transport, lower immunogenicity, and batch-to-batch reproducibility that address key limitations of cell-based approaches in aesthetic applications.14

Open in new tabDownload slideExosomes arise through a tightly choreographed endosomal route. Plasma-membrane invagination generates early endosomes whose phospholipid membrane buds inward to form intraluminal vesicles (ILVs), yielding MVBs. ILV budding can proceed through Endosomal Sorting Complex Required for Transport (ESCRT) complexes (HRS–Tsg101–ALIX–ESCRT-III) and accessory proteins, or through ESCRT-independent lipid remodeling including ceramides. Cargo (tetraspanins, receptors, enzymes, and RNAs) are sorted by ubiquitin tags, RNA-binding proteins, and lipid microdomains.15,16 Concurrently, endoplasmic reticulum (ER)–Golgi trafficking influences the membrane and protein composition available to the endosomal system, whereas Rab GTPases coordinate MVB maturation and motility.17 Mature MVBs follow 1 of 2 fates: fusion with lysosomes (cargo degradation) or trafficking to the plasma membrane for exocytosis. Docking and fusion are orchestrated by Rab27a/b and associated tethers/SNAREs; upon fusion, ILVs are expelled as exosomes into the extracellular space.18,19 Throughout this journey, cargo loading is selective—miRNAs, mRNAs, and proteins are enriched through sequence designs: a specific short stretch of nucleotides (for RNA) or amino acids that serves as a “tag” to be recognized by sorting machinery such as RNA-binding proteins (adaptors). These guide cargo into exosomes, creating vesicles optimized for paracrine signaling.20 The overall rate and composition of exosome release are stimulus responsive: hypoxia, oxidative stress, cytokines, and calcium flux shift MVB trafficking away from lysosomal degradation toward secretion, increasing vesicle output, and favoring pro-angiogenic and pro-survival payloads.21 This release is dynamic—stimuli such as hypoxia, oxidative stress, calcium influx, or cytokine exposure can trigger increased exosome secretion, enhancing communication between cells during injury, repair, or remodeling. This endo-exocytic continuum explains why exosomes display tetraspanins (CD9/CD63/CD81), and deliver curated molecular cargo to link ER/Golgi/endosome cross talk to extracellular communication during tissue repair and remodeling.22
Once released into the extracellular space, exosomes are guided toward their targets by paracrine signaling. This process functions through intercellular communication: by utilizing diffusible molecules such as growth factors, cytokines, and chemokines (the secretome), the influence remains local rather than systemic.23 In response to tissue injury or inflammation, cells release chemokines (eg, CXCL12/SDF-1) that establish a gradient and act as a homing mechanism that directs the migration of reparative cells and vesicles toward the source of injury.24
Once released into the extracellular space, exosomes travel with directional purpose rather than random drift. Their journey is guided by a combination of signaling pathways, chemokine gradients, and cell-surface recognition lures.25 This directional trafficking is also influenced by the molecular “address” that acts as a ZIP code, imprinted on exosomal membranes including tetraspanins (CD9, CD63, CD81), integrins, and adhesion molecules.26 In concert, these determine tissue tropism (the tendency of vesicles to preferentially interact with specific cell types or extracellular matrices) by binding complementary receptors on recipient-cell ligands.27 The cargo composition of the vesicle can further direct its destination. For example, exosomes enriched with angiogenic miRNAs (miR-126, miR-21) preferentially interact with endothelial cells, whereas those containing ECM-modulating proteins and integrin αvβ3 are taken up by fibroblasts.28,29 This preferential docking directs exosomes with a certain cargo toward sites where their bioactive cargo will be effective.
At the moment exosomes reach target cells, internalization occurs through multiple pathways. Ligand and receptor interactions initiate docking.30 This can trigger clathrin-mediated endocytosis, where coated pits internalize the vesicle into endosomes.31 In parallel, caveolin-mediated endocytosis through cholesterol-rich lipid rafts provides an alternative portal for smaller vesicles.32 Macropinocytosis occurs through engulfment within membrane ruffles and direct membrane fusion causes content release directly into cytoplasm. Direct membrane fusion occurs when the nanoparticles merge directly with the plasma membrane of the target cell, releasing the vesicle's cytoplasmic cargo straight into the recipient cell's cytosol without the need for endocytosis.33 This selective trafficking and uptake ensures that exosomes deliver their molecular messages precisely where repair or regeneration is needed, functioning as biological couriers that translate local injury signals into systemic regenerative responses.
Once internalized, exosomes navigate along the cell's microtubule network, passing through the endosomal system and frequently docking at the ER, where their contents are unpacked.34 This process enables their cargo to directly influence gene expression, translation and cellular homeostasis at several levels. Transferred mRNA may be translated into functional proteins and will add to the recipient cell's proteome, whereas miRNAs can silence-specific genes through the RNA-Induced Silencing Complex. In addition, lncRNA and circular RNA delivered through exosomes modulate transcriptional activity by interacting with chromatin remodelers, transcription factors, and RNA-binding proteins.35 Beyond direct gene regulation, exosome uptake can induce epigenetic remodeling, including altered DNA methylation and histone acetylation or methylation, which reprograms chromatin accessibility and creates more persistent shifts in gene expression patterns.36 Exosomes also engage intracellular tubule and vesicle systems, influencing protein trafficking between the ER, Golgi apparatus, and lysosomes, and fine-tuning autophagy and proteostasis during stress or repair.37 These layered actions—spanning translation, transcription, and epigenetic control—allow exosomes to reprogram recipient cells toward regenerative or reparative phenotypes without genomic integration or cellular engraftment.38

#### FROM CELLULAR DERIVATION TO CLINICAL VALIDATION: BRIDGING SCIENCE AND PRACTICE

Exosome derivation remains a controversial topic, encompassing scientific, commercial, ethical, and regulatory nuances. Despite the debate, the evidence is clear: exosomes are inanimate biological messengers containing nucleic acids, lipids, and proteins that influence recipient-cell behavior.39,40 If the appropriate molecular message is not transmitted, the anticipated biologic response will not occur. Recent advances in cargo characterization now allow derivation–function relationships to be understood in simple conditional terms—for example, if miR-218-5p and Wnt3a are present, β-catenin activation and dermal-papilla-cell stimulation predictably promote hair-shaft thickening and prolongation of the anagen phase.41,42 Exosomal composition is thus inextricably linked to its cellular source, which determines the specific nucleic acids, proteins, and lipids packaged within the vesicle.43-45
Adipose-derived stem-cell (ADSC) exosomes are the most prevalent in aesthetic medicine, mediating tissue regeneration through modulation of inflammation, enhancement of fibroblast proliferation, and stimulation of angiogenesis.46 Commercial manufacturing employs expanded ADSC cultures in closed bioreactors, harvesting vesicles from conditioned media. Hollow-fiber membrane systems achieve yields up to 3.5-fold higher than traditional flask culture, facilitating scalable production.47 Dermal-fibroblast-derived exosomes (HDF-Exos) offer a skin-targeted profile enriched in tissue inhibitor of metalloproteinases-1 and TGF-β with downregulated tumor-necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), increasing Procollagen I and reducing tissue metalloproteinase-1 (MMP-1) activity.48 Needle-free delivery systems have shown superior collagen deposition and anti-photoaging effects compared with bone-marrow-MSC vesicles, with ANP32B-mediated Akt activation implicated in fibroblast signaling.49 For hair regeneration, dermal-papilla-cell exosomes (DPC-Exos) convey specialized follicular signaling. Their miR-218-5p cargo modulates Wnt/β-catenin activity to trigger telogen-to-anagen conversion and follicular thickening, whereas MSC-derived vesicles upregulate vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1), promoting follicular reactivation.42 Perinatal-tissue-derived exosomes, particularly those from umbilical-cord MSCs (UCB-MSCs), possess an “age-zero” molecular environment with high EGF and wound-healing mediators, rapid skin penetration, and reduced immunogenicity—attributes that may underlie parabiosis-like rejuvenation in experimental models.50,51
To meet scalability and regulatory needs, mammalian and plant sources are increasingly explored. Bovine fetal serum and colostrum exosomes display cross-species regenerative activity through miR-1246 and HSP70 enrichment, whereas milk-derived vesicles improve skin elasticity and collagen expression in controlled human studies.52,53 Plant-derived exosomes are emerging as natural, biocompatible alternatives with antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects. *Rosa damascena* stem-cell exosomes reduce melanin and enhance fibroblast activity and grapefruit-, ginger-, apple-, and ginseng-derived vesicles promote keratinocyte migration, endothelial tube formation, and miRNA-mediated antisenescence responses, suggesting cross-kingdom regenerative potential.54-56 Autologous platelet-derived exosomes (PRP-Exos) offer immunologic safety and practical accessibility, activating YAP signaling and delivering VEGF, PDGF-BB, and TGF-β1 to enhance fibroblast proliferation and migration.57
Derivation materially determines vesicle cargo—particularly miRNAs, proteins, and lipids—and thus shapes regenerative focus. Consistent with MISEV2023 guidelines, potency should be established through functional assays rather than inferred solely from origin. Human-derived exosomes currently possess the strongest clinical support, whereas animal and plant vesicles provide scalable, complementary avenues that warrant standardized, source-controlled trials to clarify their relative therapeutic value.58

#### EXOSOME PRODUCTION, ISOLATION, CHARACTERIZATION, PURIFICATION, AND STORAGE

Transitioning from exosome science to use in clinical practice is based on 1 principle: maintaining patient safety. Although many medical specialties utilize both diagnostic and therapeutic exosomes, to date there is no regulatory clearance. Product standardization safeguards both patient and provider. The safest exosome products are manufactured under current Good Manufacturing Practice (cGMP) conditions in FDA-inspected facilities, where donor screening and documentation ensure biologic safety. Responsible manufacturers exclude donors with transmissible diseases such as HIV, hepatitis B and C, syphilis, and prion-based disorders.59,60 The growing number of unregulated “stem cell” and “exosome” formulations underscores the importance of verifying that products originate from compliant and traceable sources.61
Once donor eligibility is confirmed, exosome production begins with conditioned media—the nutrient solution used to sustain stem-cell cultures. As stem cells grow, they naturally release vesicles into this medium, which becomes the substrate for downstream isolation.62 MSCs are cultivated in bioreactors under tightly regulated conditions. These include precise control of oxygen tension, pH, and mechanical stimuli, which can influence the yield and content of secreted exosomes.63 Early studies used ultracentrifugation, which separates vesicles by density but can damage membranes and co-isolate proteins or lipoproteins. Modern manufacturing favors tangential-flow filtration, which concentrates vesicles more gently and efficiently.64 Purification by size-exclusion chromatography or density-gradient methods then removes residual proteins and soluble mediators, yielding cleaner preparations.65,66 Purity directly influences potency: the more refined the product, the lower the dose required for biologic effect.67 Multiple studies also confirm a measurable dose–response relationship, where increasing concentrations of purified exosomes proportionally enhance wound closure, angiogenesis, and collagen synthesis up to an optimal threshold.68
Each clinical batch undergoes identity and potency testing to confirm safety and function. Characterization includes vesicle size, morphology, and canonical surface markers (CD9, CD63, CD81), along with mechanism-specific bioassays such as fibroblast migration, angiogenesis, or anti-inflammatory assays.69,70 These potency-based release criteria, endorsed by the International Society for Extracellular Vesicles (ISEV), define product quality beyond particle counts and are central to therapeutic reproducibility.71
Storage stability remains a critical variable. Freezing at −80°C best preserves exosome structure and function, whereas −20°C can promote aggregation and partial loss of activity.72 Lyophilization may extend shelf life but requires protective sugars such as trehalose, sucrose, or mannitol to prevent vesicle collapse.73,74 Properly formulated products can remain stable for up to 2 years under controlled conditions, although manufacturer validation is essential.75 Claims of “shelf-stable” liquid formulations lacking cold-chain support should be viewed cautiously until verified by independent data. “Shelf-stable” formulations treated with excipients are popular skin care solutions; consumers should verify proper production and stability study validation.
Standardized, cGMP-compliant production—paired with validated isolation, purification, and storage methods—is fundamental to ensuring both patient safety and scientific integrity in exosome-based therapies.

#### EXOSOMES AND REGULATORY OVERSIGHT

Despite their growing clinical and commercial interest, no regulatory agency worldwide has yet approved an exosome product for any medical indication.76 Regulatory classification depends on the intended use. In the United States, exosome products that claim to diagnose, treat, mitigate, or prevent disease are regulated as biologics or drugs under the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (FD&C Act) and the Public Health Service (PHS) Act Section 351, requiring an Investigational New Drug (IND) application for clinical testing and a Biologics License Application (BLA) for marketing.77,78 Products that meet minimal manipulation and homologous use criteria fall under the 361 HCT/P pathway of the PHS Act and 21 CFR Part 1271.79 For cosmetic use, the FD&C Act—amended by the Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act (MoCRA, 2022)—permits only appearance-related claims; therapeutic or structural claims automatically shift the product into drug or biologic classification.80
The FDA has repeatedly warned clinics and distributors marketing unapproved “exosome” or “stem cell” injectables with therapeutic claims. These enforcement actions emphasize that most available exosome preparations (Appendix), when marketed for disease treatment, are considered unapproved biological drugs.81,82 Reported complications from such use typically arise from nonsterile or mischaracterized products, lack of cGMP compliance, or injection of minimally processed materials mislabeled as purified exosomes.83 Legitimate products require advanced isolation, purification, and validation systems operating under FDA or equivalent GMP oversight.84
Regulatory approaches outside the United States show broad alignment. Within the European Union, exosome-based therapeutics are regulated as Advanced Therapy Medicinal Products under Regulation (EC) No 1394/2007, requiring GMP production and validated testing for identity, sterility, and potency in accordance with Annex 2 of EudraLex Volume 4.85,86 The UK Medicines and Healthcare Products Regulatory Agency (MHRA) and Health Canada have adopted similar frameworks treating exosome formulations as biologic substances subject to batch reproducibility and defined potency assays.87 Japan's Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Agency and Korea's Ministry of Food and Drug Safety have established accelerated review routes for exosome-containing cosmetics and regenerative injectables, provided that traceability, nonsystemic action, and microbial safety are demonstrated.88
Across all jurisdictions, 3 regulatory priorities have emerged: source accountability, documenting donor provenance and ethical cell-line derivation; process transparency, demonstrating compliance with Good Manufacturing Practice/Current Good Tissue Practice (GMP/CGTP) standards and in-process controls; and analytic traceability, confirming product identity, purity, and potency using validated, ISEV-endorsed assays.67 Together, these standards aim to balance innovation with patient safety, establishing the foundation for reproducible, legally compliant use of exosomes in regenerative and aesthetic medicine.

#### EXOSOMES IN SKIN REJUVENATION

The foundation for exosome-based skin rejuvenation is supported by coordinated bench, animal, and clinical research. In vitro, UCB-MSC exosomes increased Type I collagen and elastin synthesis while suppressing MMP-1 expression in human fibroblasts, restoring extracellular-matrix organization.50 Adipose-derived vesicles similarly reversed UV-induced fibroblast senescence by normalizing MAPK/AP-1 signaling, reducing oxidative stress, and rebalancing collagen-to-MMP ratios.89 Collectively, these studies confirm that exosomal RNA and protein cargo can reverse age-related cellular dysfunction.
In vivo models reinforce these findings. Application of human dermal-fibroblast exosomes to UV-exposed SKH-1 hairless mice reduced wrinkle depth, transepidermal water loss, and inflammation while restoring elastin and epidermal barrier integrity.90 Likewise, human UCB-MSC extracellular vesicles prevented photoaging more effectively than topical retinoic acid, reinstating COL1A1 and SIRT3 expression and reducing oxidative DNA damage.91
Clinical evidence mirrors preclinical outcomes. In a double-blind split-face trial, fractional CO2 laser resurfacing combined with adipose-stem-cell exosomes improved acne-scar severity by 32.5% vs 19.9% with laser alone and enhanced skin texture.92 A 6-week prospective study of topical platelet-derived exosomes reported visible improvement in tone, elasticity, and photodamage with excellent tolerability.93 In another clinical trial, topical application of conditioned-media-derived exosomes improved fine lines, pigmentation, and hydration in photoaged skin without adverse effects.94
Common delivery approaches include microinjection, microneedling, and topical application after energy-based procedures. Energy-based devices (EBDs) “prime” the skin by inducing heat shock proteins and adhesion molecules that enhance vesicle uptake.95 Nonthermal radiofrequency microneedling at 1 to 1.5 mm depth followed by topical exosome application uses transient microchannels to increase penetration.96 Although the FDA classifies exosomes as unapproved biologics, practitioners have adopted microinjection protocols (0.02-0.05 mL per site in a grid pattern) to target photodamage, rosacea, and chronic inflammation, typically over 2 to 3 sessions at 4-week intervals with semiannual maintenance.97

#### EXOSOMES IN SCAR MANAGEMENT

Bench studies demonstrate that ADSC exosomes mitigate fibrosis by downregulating profibrotic signaling in hypertrophic and keloid fibroblasts. Their miR-125b-5p cargo suppresses Smad2 and reduces α-SMA, COL1, and COL3 expression.98 ADSC-Exos also inhibit PI3K/AKT/mTOR activity, restore mitochondrial autophagy, and decrease inflammatory cytokine release.99 Conversely, exosomes from keloid patients are enriched in circular RNAs that activate the TGF-β1/Smad2/3 pathway in normal fibroblasts—highlighting a pathological vesicle-mediated loop that therapeutic exosomes can counter.98
In vivo studies support these antifibrotic mechanisms. In rabbit-ear hypertrophic scar models, ADSC-Exos enriched in miR-194 suppress TGF-β1, lower inflammatory mediators, and reduce collagen density and scar thickness.100 An exosome-loaded gelatin methacryloyl hydrogel sustained local delivery and markedly decreased COL1, COL3, α-SMA, and VEGF expression after 28 days compared with controls.101 In human keloid xenograft mice, ADSC-Exos restored autophagy, normalized collagen architecture, and reduced fibrosis in vivo.101
Human clinical evidence, although still emerging, is encouraging. In a prospective cohort, Chernoff reported that 18 of 21 patients treated for keloids with excision followed by topical exosome therapy and amniotic membrane overlay remained recurrence free at 2 years.102 A systematic review of early human studies found consistent improvements in acne, surgical, and procedural scars with exosome adjunct therapy, although high-quality randomized trials for hypertrophic and keloid scars remain limited.103
Clinically, exosomes may be applied topically, through microinjection, or enhanced by EBDs. For postoperative scars, early intervention 7-14 days postsurgery using intradermal microinjections (0.01-0.02 mL per point, 0.5 cm spacing) along the incision line is most effective.7 Established scars respond best to microneedling (1.5-2.0 mm depth) followed by topical exosome application, performed in 3 to 4 sessions at 4- to 6-week intervals, with maintenance every 6 to 12 months.104 Ablative fractional laser resurfacing immediately followed by topical exosomes maximizes dermal penetration and accelerates remodeling.105

#### EXOSOMES IN POSTPROCEDURE RECOVERY

Exosomes have shown substantial promise in accelerating cutaneous recovery following EBD procedures. In vitro studies reveal that keratinocyte-derived extracellular vesicles activate MAPK/ERK/p38 signaling, enhancing fibroblast and keratinocyte migration and proliferation—key mechanisms of postlaser re-epithelialization.106 Human dermal-fibroblast exosomes similarly increase procollagen synthesis, elastin expression, and barrier-protein restoration under thermal and oxidative stress conditions, supporting recovery from ablative and nonablative procedures.107
Animal models corroborate these findings: topical MSC exosomes applied after fractional laser injury promote faster epidermal regeneration, increase angiogenic markers (VEGF, CD31), and suppress interleukin-6 and TNF-α compared with untreated controls.108
Clinical studies confirm these regenerative benefits. In a double-blind split-face randomized controlled trial (RCT), topical adipose-stem-cell exosomes applied immediately after fractional CO2 resurfacing improved acne-scar severity and reduced downtime vs control gel.109 A 2024 randomized trial found that an exosome hydrogel used postfractional CO2 laser accelerated healing, reduced erythema, and improved patient comfort compared with standard silicone-gel care.110 Another controlled trial demonstrated that exosome-enriched post-EBD regimens improved periorbital texture, tone, and pigmentation relative to baseline.111
Clinically, cGMP-verified exosome formulations are applied immediately postprocedure and continued for 7 to 14 days to minimize erythema, transepidermal water loss, and crusting.112 Postablative fractional laser or radiofrequency microneedling protocols typically employ a single in-office application followed by at-home maintenance, integrating easily into existing EBD recovery regimens.113 This combination of procedural synergy and enhanced patient recovery has made exosome postcare one of the fastest-growing segments in aesthetic practice.
The regenerative mechanism of exosome therapy in hair restoration centers on reactivation of dermal papilla cells and modulation of the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, prolonging the anagen phase and partially reversing follicular miniaturization.41 In vitro, exosomal miR-218-5p suppresses SFRP2 (a Wnt antagonist), thereby activating β-catenin and up-regulating vascular and growth factors including VEGF and IGF-1, which enhance follicular angiogenesis and keratinocyte proliferation.114,115 In a nude-mouse skin-reconstitution assay, supplementation of 50 µg/mL ADSC exosomes to dermal–epidermal grafts increased regenerated-follicle counts, produced more mature terminal shafts, and shifted the microenvironment toward pro-angiogenic signaling (↑PDGF, ↑VEGF) with concomitant ↓TGF-β1—a pattern consistent with anagen entry and fibrosis restraint.114 In a murine wound-induced folliculogenesis model, dermal-papilla-derived exosomes accelerated re-epithelialization and de novo follicle formation through β-catenin/Lef1 activation; these effects were abrogated by Wnt inhibition, confirming pathway dependence.115
Clinical evidence substantiates these experimental findings. In a double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of 39 adults treated with topical human platelet exosome serum, trichoscopic analysis demonstrated increased hair density by 11.3% at 3 months and 13.1% at 9 months, with concurrent improvements in hair width (+15.4%) and follicular units (+10.2%) without adverse events.116 In a prospective study of foreskin-derived MSC exosomes injected intradermally in 30 men with androgenetic alopecia, mean hair density increased from 128 to 162 hairs/cm2 (frontal) and 121 to 133 hairs/cm2 (vertex) by Week 12 ( *P* < .05), accompanied by enhanced patient satisfaction and no complications. Ersan et al reported terminal-hair counts rising from 96.5 ± 12.3 to 163.5 ± 15.8 hairs/cm2 6 weeks posttreatment, with 20.4% thickening and decreased shedding.117 Similarly, Lee et al observed in 27 female patients that adipose-derived stem-cell conditioned medium increased follicular density from 105.4 to 122.7 follicles/cm2 ( *P* < .001) and shaft thickness from 57.5 to 64.0 µm ( *P* < .001), without serious adverse events.118
Clinical implementation typically employs intradermal microinjection targeting the follicular bulge and dermal papilla. Despite FDA advisories, off-label use is widespread. The standard nappage-style technique delivers ∼0.05 to 0.1 mL per site at ∼1 cm spacing, totaling 3 to 5 mL per session.119 Topical anesthesia or ring block enhances comfort. Treatment protocols generally involve 3 to 4 sessions at 4- to 6-week intervals, with maintenance boosters every 6 to 12 months. Clinical improvement becomes evident within 6 to 12 weeks as telogen follicles transition to anagen through exosome-mediated Wnt activation.120 Adjunctive regimens combining exosomes with finasteride, dutasteride, minoxidil (topical or oral), photodynamic therapy, and nutritional supplementation yield synergistic results.120

#### WOUND HEALING

Exosome-enhanced wound healing is primarily mediated through paracrine cross talk between mesenchymal stem cells, fibroblasts, keratinocytes, and endothelial cells, which stimulates migration, proliferation, angiogenesis, and extracellular-matrix synthesis.121 In vitro, mesenchymal-cell exosomes accelerate fibroblast proliferation and migration while promoting angiogenic tube formation through activation of the PI3K/AKT pathway and upregulation of pro-matrix genes including COL1A1, COL3A1, and PCNA.122 Adipose-derived vesicles further demonstrate dose-dependent increases in collagen deposition and wound closure, with pathway inhibition confirming PI3K/AKT dependence.123 Cooperative activation of Wnt/β-catenin and TGF-β signaling promotes cellular proliferation, organized collagen remodeling, and tempering of inflammatory responses.124
Animal studies validate these findings in vivo. Umbilical-cord-blood exosomes accelerate full-thickness wound closure through miR-21-3p-mediated angiogenesis and fibroblast activation, increasing endothelial and proliferative markers within granulation tissue.125 Platelet-derived extracellular-vesicle hydrogels sustain local vesicle release and improve re-epithelialization, neovascularization, and collagen organization compared with controls.126 Hydrogen-peroxide–preconditioned adipose-MSC exosomes enhance ischemic flap survival through PI3K/AKT-mediated angiogenesis and apoptosis reduction.127
Clinical studies corroborate these regenerative effects. In a randomized, double-blind trial, Wharton's jelly MSC exosome gel significantly increased complete-closure rates and reduced healing time in diabetic foot ulcers vs standard care, with excellent tolerability.128 Case applications have demonstrated complete epithelial restoration of ischemic flaps and chronic radiated wounds after serial topical exosome therapy.129,130 Histologic and imaging studies reveal that exosomes can induce full dermal regeneration (also shown in Figures 2, 3) rather than simple epithelial resurfacing, a process attributed to activation of the discoidin domain receptor-1 pathway—an ECM-integrin axis that promotes fibroblast differentiation, coordinated collagen alignment, and restoration of native dermal architecture. Other cell signaling pathways act in concert to assist in dermal regeneration.122,131

#### EXOSOMES IN FAT GRAFT ENHANCEMENT

Exosomes enhance adipose tissue regeneration and graft survival through coordinated promotion of adipogenesis, angiogenesis, and modulation of inflammation.135 In vitro studies show that ADSC exosomes stimulate endothelial proliferation, migration, and tube formation through upregulation of VEGF, effects intensified under hypoxic preconditioning through a let-7/AGO1/VEGF signaling axis that supports early graft revascularization.136 Complementary data demonstrate increased preadipocyte differentiation through PPARγ activation and enhanced pro-angiogenic signaling, confirming exosomes as principal paracrine mediators of adipose regeneration.137
Animal models corroborate these mechanisms: co-transplantation of ADSC exosomes with lipoaspirate improves graft volume retention, microvessel density, and adipocyte viability compared with controls, with hypoxia-derived vesicles showing superior effects through VEGF/VEGFR and ANG-1/Tie2 signaling.138,139 These findings establish exosomes as facilitators of neovascularization and extracellular-matrix remodeling during graft integration.
Human data, while limited, align with preclinical outcomes. Systematic reviews indicate that extracellular-vesicle-enriched grafts demonstrate higher long-term retention and vascularization, and related randomized trials of adipose-derived stem-cell-enriched fat grafts show superior residual volume and perfusion in reconstructive and aesthetic applications.140,141
Clinically, exosomes are incorporated into autologous fat transfer by gently mixing purified, GMP-characterized preparations with harvested lipoaspirate before reinjection. Preconditioning enhances inosculation and long-term survival. Micro-aliquot, multi-plane delivery optimizes surface area and integration, whereas irrigation of the recipient bed or a perimetric “halo” of exosome suspension augments vascular ingrowth and early graft stability.142,143
Currently, no FDA-approved indications exist for exosome-based therapies. Clinicians seeking biologically compliant adjuncts may use autologous secretome sources such as stromal-vascular-fraction-enhanced fat grafts, which provide endogenous extracellular vesicles and cytokines, or platelet-derived preparations including platelet-rich plasma and platelet-rich fibrin, which are minimally manipulated and legally permissible under current US guidelines.144,145 These methods optimize graft survival while remaining within regulatory boundaries.

#### ADVANTAGES AND LIMITATIONS OF EXOSOMES IN AESTHETIC MEDICINE

Exosomes offer several advantages in aesthetic medicine, combining biological potency with minimal invasiveness and broad therapeutic versatility.146 Their nanoscale size, biocompatibility, and pleiotropic mechanisms allow targeted modulation of inflammation, oxidative stress, angiogenesis, and extracellular-matrix remodeling with natural-appearing results and short recovery times. Through coordinated delivery of miRNAs, proteins, and lipids, exosomes simultaneously address multiple pathways of cellular aging and repair, making them well suited for integrative rejuvenation protocols across skin, hair, and soft-tissue applications.
Despite these advantages, important limitations persist. Clinical performance varies with the source of parental cells, upstream conditioning, and nonstandardized isolation or purification methods, complicating reproducibility and cross-study comparison.146 Stability remains formulation dependent—requiring validated cold-chain or lyophilized storage—and potency assays are not yet harmonized across manufacturers. Regulatory frameworks also remain fluid: most therapeutic claims currently fall under FDA biologic or drug pathways requiring IND or BLA level manufacturing and validated chemistry, manufacturing, and control data, creating uncertainty and limiting accessibility.147 Cost further restricts widespread adoption, as production of GMP-grade vesicle formulations remains resource intensive.
Rapid progress in closed-system bioreactors, standardized quantification metrics (particle/protein ratios, orthogonal analytics), and advanced delivery systems is addressing these gaps.148 Concurrently, maturing regulatory guidance and field consensus on potency and purity standards are moving exosome-based aesthetics from experimental adoption toward standardized, predictable clinical protocols. Because these scientific, logistical, and regulatory challenges converge, exosomes are poised to transition from emerging biologics to a defined category within mainstream aesthetic medicine.

#### THE FUTURE OF EXOSOMES IN AESTHETICS: ENGINEERING NEW FRONTIERS

The future of exosome therapy in aesthetics lies in bioengineering strategies designed to overcome current limitations and enhance regenerative precision.149 Four innovations are expected to redefine clinical practice. First, programmable cargo loading enables selective enrichment of vesicles with miRNAs, growth factors, and antioxidants that amplify photoprotection, stimulate collagen synthesis, and mitigate inflammation without irritation. Second, surface-targeting modification uses tissue-specific peptides and ligands to direct exosomes toward precise anatomical sites—epidermis, follicular bulge, or adipose niches—enhancing delivery efficiency and minimizing off-target effects. Third, hybrid biomaterial–exosome composites combine vesicles with hydrogels or scaffolds for slow, spatially controlled release, sustaining regenerative signaling and improving graft integration and durability.149 Finally, advanced bioprocessing platforms, including closed-system bioreactors and microfluidic purification, are reducing production costs by up to 80% while improving reproducibility and functional consistency.150
As artificial-intelligence-driven analytics and multi-omics profiling (genomic, proteomic, and microbiome mapping) become integrated into exosome design, truly personalized regenerative protocols will emerge.151 Vesicle cargo and surface characteristics may soon be tailored to each patient's molecular aging signature, anatomical region, and aesthetic objective. This convergence of bioengineering, biomaterials science, and computational biology heralds a new era of precision regenerative aesthetics—one in which exosome therapeutics evolve from broad biologic adjuncts to customized, indication-specific tools spanning skin rejuvenation, scar modulation, hair restoration, wound healing, and volumetric enhancement.152

#### CONCLUSIONS

Exosomes are reshaping the landscape of aesthetic and regenerative medicine by merging molecular precision with clinical artistry. Their capacity to modulate multiple biological pathways simultaneously—repairing tissue, recalibrating inflammation, and re-establishing youthful cellular communication—positions them as the cornerstone of next-generation rejuvenation protocols. The convergence of rigorous bench science, validated animal models, and expanding human clinical evidence supports their application across skin, hair, scar, and adipose therapies with a degree of biologic sophistication not achievable through conventional modalities.
Because manufacturing platforms mature and regulatory frameworks gain clarity, exosomes are poised to evolve from experimental adjuncts into standardized therapeutic tools defined by measurable potency and reproducibility. The field's advancement will rely on harmonizing scientific rigor with practical implementation, ensuring accessibility without compromising safety or quality. Ultimately, exosome-based therapies signify a new paradigm in aesthetic medicine—one that restores form and function not through external alteration, but by reactivating the body's own regenerative intelligence. This biologic precision offers natural, durable, and patient-specific outcomes that blur the traditional boundaries between science and beauty.
